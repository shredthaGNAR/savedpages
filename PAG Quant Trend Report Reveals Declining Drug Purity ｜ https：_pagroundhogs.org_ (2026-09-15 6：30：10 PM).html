PAG Quant Trend Report Reveals Declining Drug Purity

July 10, 2025|ADULTERANTS, Drug Discoveries, Trends

[PHILADELPHIA]   PA GROUNDHOGS—Pennsylvania’s statewide lab-based drug checking organization—has released its inaugural Drug Purity Report, showing changes in drug purity from January 2023 to May 2025. The report analyzed quantitative data for fentanyl, methamphetamine, cocaine and xylazine, and includes the most comprehensive data published to date on the extent of medetomidine adulteration in the illicit fentanyl supply.

Between January 2023 and May 2025 PA GROUNDHOGS and its partner lab, the Center for Forensic Science, Research & Education (CFSRE) analyzed 1,038 drug samples. 

Key takeaways include:

  1. Methamphetamine, fentanyl, and xylazine are all experiencing statistically significant declines in reported average purity since 2023.
  2. Cocaine purity, on average, has remained effectively unchanged, with no meaningful trend observed.
  3. However, Pennsylvania’s drug supply (particularly cocaine) is highly volatile with purity sometimes rising and falling by wide margins from one month to the next.            

Such market volatility in illicit drug purity — especially when strength, composition, or adulterants change unpredictably— has significant consequences for consumer safety. Market volatility creates a minefield for drug users, even those who use cautiously. It undermines harm reduction, increases overdose risk, and complicates medical response — all while staying invisible to the untrained eye.

PA GROUNDHOGS’ Quantitative Trend Analysis (2023-2025) spans 11 pages and includes 10 revealing charts and graphs unpacking the ramifications of purity variability on harm reduction, consumer expectations and clinical responses to overdose and withdrawal.
