- **Q4_0**: Small size with very high quality loss. This is a legacy option. It's recommended to use **Q3_K_M** for better quality instead.
  
- **Q4_1**: Small size with substantial quality loss. This is another legacy option, and **Q3_K_L** is considered a better alternative.

- **Q5_0**: Medium size with balanced quality. Though this is a legacy option, it's recommended to use **Q4_K_M** for a better balance of size and performance.

- **Q5_1**: Medium size with low quality loss. It’s a legacy option, but **Q5_K_M** is a more reliable and modern choice.

- **Q2_K**: The smallest option with extreme quality loss. This quantization type is not recommended due to its significant degradation in quality.

- **Q3_K_S**: Very small size, but it comes with very high quality loss. It’s suitable only for highly constrained environments.

- **Q3_K_M**: Very small size with very high quality loss, but still a recommended option for those needing compact models with manageable quality loss.

- **Q3_K_L**: Small size with noticeable quality loss. This option works well when space is a priority but quality cannot be significantly sacrificed.

- **Q4_K_S**: Small size with significant quality loss. While usable, there are better alternatives for a balanced quality-to-size trade-off.

- **Q4_K_M**: Medium size with balanced quality. This is a recommended option, offering a great trade-off between size and quality, making it ideal for many scenarios.

- **Q5_K_S**: Large size with low quality loss. This is one of the recommended choices for users seeking higher quality without significantly increasing the model size.

- **Q5_K_M**: Large size with very low quality loss. This is a highly recommended option for those who want minimal quality loss and can handle the larger model size.

- **Q6_K**: Very large size with extremely low quality loss. Ideal for scenarios where quality is paramount and size is less of a concern.

- **Q8_0**: Very large size with almost no quality loss, but not recommended due to the disproportionate size compared to the minimal gain in quality.

- **F16**: Extremely large size with virtually no quality loss. Not recommended as the size is impractical for most use cases.

- **F32**: Absolutely huge size with perfect quality retention, but it’s not recommended because the size requirements are excessive for most applications.
